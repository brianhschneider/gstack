# VC Investment Intelligence: 2024-2026

*Researched May 2026. Covers 2024 through early 2026 activity.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

### Macro Trends

**2025 was a historic year for venture capital, driven almost entirely by AI.** Total global VC deployment reached $425 billion, up 30% from $328 billion in 2024. AI-specific VC investment hit $211 billion in 2025, up 85% year-over-year from $114 billion in 2024. The OECD confirmed that in 2025, AI firms captured 61% ($258.7 billion) of all global VC investment.

The defining feature of the era: a small number of foundational AI companies absorbed unprecedented capital. OpenAI alone raised $40 billion in a single round (March 2025), then disclosed plans for another $10+ billion, with total funding approaching $120+ billion. Anthropic closed a $30 billion round in February 2026. xAI raised $20 billion in October 2025. These are not outliers — they are the new benchmark for "mega-round."

**Capital concentration is extreme.** Rounds of $100M+ now dominate, with mega-deals ($1B+) becoming common. A total of 15 companies secured $2 billion or more in single rounds in 2025. Early-stage deal count is falling while check sizes are swelling. The message: VCs are making fewer but larger bets on category leaders.

### Hottest Categories (in order of capital deployed)

1. **Foundational AI / LLMs** — OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Mistral, Cohere
2. **AI infrastructure** — Databricks, Crusoe, Lambda, GPU cloud
3. **Defense tech** — Anduril, Shield AI, Saronic, Hadrian; $49.1B in defense-related funding in 2025 (nearly double 2024's $27.2B)
4. **AI coding and developer tools** — Cursor/Anysphere, Cognition, Harvey
5. **Vertical AI applications** — Sierra, Glean, Hebbia, Perplexity
6. **Robotics/humanoids** — Figure AI, Apptronik, 1X Technologies
7. **Cybersecurity** — $18B invested in 2025, up 26%
8. **Nuclear energy** — CFS, Helion, TAE; 44% of energy VC captured by fusion/fission
9. **Fintech** — $51.8B globally in 2025
10. **Longevity/biotech** — Retro Biosciences, NewLimit, Altos Labs

### Most Active VC Firms by Deal Volume (2025)

Y Combinator (via batch investments), Andreessen Horowitz ($15B+ raised across 6 funds in January 2026), Sequoia Capital (~$56B AUM, 126 investments in 2025), Lightspeed Venture Partners ($9B new capital, December 2025), Accel (147 investments in 2025), Bessemer (102 investments in 2025), Khosla Ventures (104 investments in 2025).

## 6. Emerging Themes and Investment Theses (2025-2026)

### 1. Vertical AI / Domain-Specific Agents
The era of "one model for everything" is yielding to specialized AI trained on curated, high-quality domain data. VCs increasingly prefer companies with proprietary data moats — legal (Harvey, Hebbia), medical (insitro, OpenEvidence), finance. The thesis: general-purpose models are commoditized utilities; domain specialists own the margin.

### 2. Agentic AI at Work
Cursor's $2B ARR in 3 years proved that AI agents can replace significant human labor in real workflows. Sierra's $100M ARR in under 2 years proved it in customer service. The next frontier: AI agents that don't just assist but fully own tasks. Capital is concentrating on companies that can demonstrate measurable labor replacement with acceptable quality.

### 3. Physical AI / Embodied Intelligence
Robotics is re-rated as a software problem. Companies like Skild AI (foundation model for robots), Wayve (foundation model for vehicles), and Apptronik treat the body as hardware and the real investment as software/AI. Humanoid robots are 2-3 years from meaningful production volume. VCs see this as a 10-year bet but are writing large checks now.

### 4. Defense Tech Goes Mainstream
For the first time, top-tier VCs (a16z, Sequoia, Lightspeed, Founders Fund) are running toward defense, not away. Anduril's $61B valuation and Palantir's $160B+ public market cap validated the category. The thesis: the US defense industrial base needs a complete software upgrade; only Silicon Valley can do it at software speed. Shield Capital, In-Q-Tel, Lux Defense Leaders are specialist funds; generalist VCs are following.

### 5. Nuclear Energy for AI
Data centers are the limiting factor for AI — they need power 24/7 at gigawatt scale. Renewables can't do it alone. Nuclear — both existing plants and fusion/SMRs — has become a strategic investment for AI infrastructure. Hyperscalers are signing power purchase agreements with nuclear plants; VCs are funding fusion (CFS, Helion) and SMR startups. Microsoft's Helion deal (50MW PPA) is the template.

### 6. Sovereign AI
Outside the US, nations and corporations increasingly want AI models they can run without depending on American hyperscalers. Mistral (France), Cohere (enterprise sovereign), and Helsing (European defense AI) represent this trend. VCs in Europe and the Middle East are investing specifically in "sovereign AI" capabilities. a16z's American Dynamism is the US analog — keeping AI capability domestic.

### 7. AI Infrastructure Capital Consolidation
GPU clouds (Crusoe, Lambda), AI platforms (Databricks), and data infrastructure (Scale AI) are receiving late-stage capital at sovereign fund scale. The check writers are no longer traditional VCs — they're SoftBank, GIC (Singapore), Mubadala (UAE), MGX (UAE), PSP (Canada). This globalization of AI capital is a defining feature of 2024-2026.

### 8. Crypto's AI Moment
After the 2022 collapse, crypto VC is back — but the narrative has shifted. The hottest crypto investments now involve AI intersections: decentralized compute (for AI inference), AI-generated content authenticity, crypto payments for AI services. Paradigm's expansion into AI and robotics signals that the best crypto investors see the two domains converging.

### 9. Longevity Goes Institutional
Longevity biotech has shifted from fringe billionaire hobby to mainstream VC category. $8.49B in 2024 deals, up from $3.82B in 2023. Retro Biosciences ($1B Series A), NewLimit ($175M), and Altos Labs ($3B) represent serious capital bets on extending human healthspan. The key scientific bet: cellular reprogramming (Yamanaka factors) and epigenetic reversal.

### 10. AI + Fintech = "Autonomous Finance"
Ramp's success demonstrates that AI can run corporate financial operations with minimal human involvement. The new thesis is not "AI-assisted finance" but "AI-native autonomous finance" — where AI sets the budget, flags anomalies, pays vendors, and reconciles accounts. Ramp's trajectory from corporate cards to $1B ARR autonomous finance platform is the template every fintech investor is studying.
